## 2. Maps 05 and 06: a refused take-off bans the zone for good

### Mechanism

In the expansion loop, a restricted neighbour goes through two checks, and the zone is marked
visited between them:

1. the general check (lines 169-175): not visited, connection free at t, zone free at t+1;
2. `visited.add(next_node)` (line 177);
3. the landing check (lines 190-197): zone free at t+2. When it fails:
   `can_wait = True; continue`.

When step 3 refuses, the zone stays in `visited` although the drone never took off. From then
on, every expansion that looks at that zone rejects it at step 1 (`next_node in visited`). And
because `all_visited` now counts it as visited, the wait rule (line 222,
`can_wait and not all_visited`) stops the drone from waiting in front of it. Once the only way
forward leads through that zone, the search runs out of states.

Before `ec0da55` the landing check read t+1 and almost never refused, which hid this. Report 1
predicted it (section 3.3, experiment E1): making the landing check correct without fixing
`visited` turns capacity violations into "Path not found".

### Trace, map 05, drone D1

D0 holds `r` at t2. `r` holds one drone, and `start-r` has capacity 3.

| Turn | Expansion | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `start`: `start-r` passes the general check (`r` is free at t1, since D0 is in flight then) | `r` added to `visited` |
| 0 | landing check: `r` at t2 is 1 >= 1 | refused. `all_visited` was computed before step 2, so it is False and D1 waits at `start` |
| 1 | `start`: `r` is in `visited` | rejected. Every neighbour is visited, so no wait |
| - | queue empty | "Path not found" |

The program's own debug output for D1 shows the single wait at turn 0 and nothing after it:

```
------ Previous nodes ------
0, start: start (using None)
------ Reconstructing path ------
```

Map 06 follows the same pattern, with D1 routing through `b`. The take-off from `b` towards
`r` is refused at t1 (`r` is full at t3), which marks `r` visited. After that, neither `b` nor
`a` may try `r` again, and neither may wait, because all their neighbours are visited.

### Confirmation

Experiment F1, on a copy only: remove `next_node` from `visited` when the landing check refuses.

| | Baseline | F1 |
|---|---|---|
| map 05 | Path not found | valid, 7 turns |
| map 06 | Path not found | valid, 6 turns |
| map 10 | Path not found | Path not found |
| random maps OK (seed 7 / seed 8) | 1,675 / 1,698 | 1,804 / 1,801 |

The fix only needs the zone to stay unvisited until a take-off is accepted: the landing check
has to come before `visited.add`, or at least before the zone is committed.

## 3. Map 10: the search stops before the drone reaches a restricted goal

### Mechanism

After a neighbour is accepted, lines 212-214 run whatever kind of move it is:

```
if next_node == map.exit_point:
    drone.done = True
    break
```

For a normal exit, the exit is reached on the next turn, and `prev_nodes[(turn, exit)]`
was written just above, so stopping right away works.

For a restricted exit, the accepted move is a take-off. Two states are queued: the flight at
t+1 and the landing at t+2. `drone.done` stops the `while` loop before either is popped, so:

- the flight is never popped, so `prev_nodes[(t+1, exit)] = (connection, None)` (line 218) is
  never written;
- `end_turn` is the take-off turn t, and reconstruction looks up `(t, exit)`, a key that
  cannot exist, because the drone is in the goal only at t+2.

Map 10's debug output shows the take-off recorded and reconstruction starting one step too
early:

```
------ Previous nodes ------
0, a: start (using start-a)
1, a-goal: a (using a-goal)
1, a: a (using None)
------ Reconstructing path ------
1 goal
Failed to find solution
```

The pop-time check at line 121 (`if current == map.exit_point: break`) would be the right
place to stop. It is never reached for a normal exit, because the push-time stop fires first.
For a restricted exit, it would need to set `end_turn` itself: `end_turn` is only updated at
the end of an iteration (line 238), and the key reconstruction needs is `(landing turn - 1,
exit)`.

This case accounts for most of the random-map failures, because the generator often makes the
goal restricted.

### Confirmation

Experiment F2, on a copy only: skip the push-time stop when the exit is restricted, and at
line 121 set `end_turn = turn - 1` before breaking.

| | Baseline | F2 only | F1 + F2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| map 10 | Path not found | valid, 3 turns | valid, 3 turns |
| maps 05, 06 | Path not found | Path not found | valid, 7 and 6 turns |
| random maps OK (seed 7 / seed 8) | 1,675 / 1,698 | 2,797 / 2,813 | **3,000 / 3,000** |

With F1 and F2 together, every restricted map, every provided map and all 6,000 random maps
produce valid schedules.

## 4. Remaining cost: the t+1 zone check on restricted destinations

This is not a failure, but it explains why F1 + F2 give 7 and 6 turns on maps 05 and 06
instead of 5.

The general check (line 172) still compares the destination's occupancy at t+1 with its
capacity, for every neighbour. For a restricted destination, the drone is in flight at t+1,
not in the zone. So a zone that is busy on the flight turn but free on the landing turn still
refuses the take-off. In map 05, D1 cannot take off at t1 (landing at t3, when `r` is empty)
because D0 is still in `r` at t2.

Experiment F3 (with F1 + F2): skip the t+1 zone comparison when the destination is
restricted, and keep only the t+2 landing check. Maps 05 and 06 both drop to 5 turns, the
expected values. All maps and all 6,000 random maps stay valid.

## 5. Summary

| Failure | Cause | Where | Fix confirmed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| 05, 06 | zone marked visited before the landing check can refuse the take-off; the refusal then also disables waiting | lines 177 and 190-197, wait rule line 222 | F1 |
| 10 | search stops when the take-off towards a restricted exit is queued; the landing is never recorded and `end_turn` is the take-off turn | lines 212-214, 121, 238 | F2 |
| 05, 06 extra turns | zone capacity checked at t+1 for restricted destinations | line 172 | F3 |

Both failures come from the same pattern already noted in report 1: `visited` and the stop
condition are driven by the moment a move is considered (queued), not by when it is accepted
(`visited`) or completed (stop). With 1-turn moves the three moments coincide. A restricted
move pulls them apart: acceptance can fail after the checks start, and completion comes two
turns after queuing.

## 6. Other observations

- The two states queued for a take-off share one `insertion_idx` (lines 203-211). It is
  harmless because their turns differ, but the landing state (t+2) is independent of the flight
  state (t+1). Its predecessor link only exists because the flight is popped first, which
  holds since the heap orders by turn.
- The landing state is queued with `(None, None)`, so after landing the drone may immediately
  take the connection it arrived on back the other way. The previous version skipped it
  (`connection == transit_connection`). This is not a correctness issue: going back costs turns
  and the search never picks it.
- flake8 on `simulation.py`: `dis.Instruction` is imported but unused (F401), and several lines
  are over 79 characters (E501, lines 104, 186, 195, 206 and others).

The experimental copies (F1, F2, F3) live only in the session scratchpad; they isolate causes
and are not proposed code.
